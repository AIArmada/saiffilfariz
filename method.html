Diagnostic · Getting to the real problem

When a client says:

“We need AI training.”

That sentence often hides several different problems: unclear use cases, weak workflow, inaccessible knowledge, mismatched expectations, low confidence, noisy tooling, or a room that lacks shared language. My job is to expose the real problem before pretending to solve it.

Field notes · What I believe this quarter

THE MAP SHOULD
change the room.

01

A prompt is not a workflow. A workflow is not yet a system.

A clever prompt can be a useful start, but real impact comes from intentional workflows, and real change comes from well-designed systems.

02

Technology should not merely get more powerful. People should.

The point is not to chase newer tools, but to build more capable, confident and creative people who can use them well.

03

Do not just learn AI. Learn better because AI exists.

Treat AI as a catalyst for better questions, sharper thinking and more meaningful work — not just a faster way to do the same old things.
